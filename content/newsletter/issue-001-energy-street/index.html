One result, then the work behind it

The sloshing CFD study reproduced the tank's first-mode frequency within about 0.30 % of linear theory across the tested fill levels. Read the study. It also includes a SPHERIC Test 10 impact-pressure check; frequency agreement alone does not establish accurate impact pressures. The result applies to the published two-dimensional tank cases. The analysis is reviewed against available public literature. A different tank, fill level or forcing will need its own inputs and checks. The report keeps the convergence evidence beside the result so the reader can inspect the basis.

Ask big, then check the work

Give your AI assistant an outcome, a bounded scope and a clear definition of done: define the must-have result. Start with one case. Check the inputs, units, first calculation and saved output before expanding the work. For a longer task, agree on a checkpoint and look for completed cases, recorded exceptions and results read back from the actual output. Check results against a hand calculation or a published benchmark, which gives a stronger quality assessment. Keep an engineer responsible for the assumptions and the decision. Ask for missing evidence when a claim cannot be checked. Fixed units, clear criteria, repeatable checks and an independent review keep the engineering precise. The aim is useful work that can be checked and repeated.
