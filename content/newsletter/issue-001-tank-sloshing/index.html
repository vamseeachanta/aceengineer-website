The formula and the CFD agree at small roll

Sloshing matters in tanks on floating vessels. Near resonance, the liquid runs up the tank walls and strikes them. A partly filled tank tuned near a vessel's roll period can also act as an anti-roll damper. Both uses start from the tank's first sloshing frequency, the frequency at which the liquid swings most easily from wall to wall. This article gives that frequency from a closed-form formula and from open-source computational fluid dynamics (CFD). It shows how far the resonance moves at larger roll angles, and it names two checks, the roll angle and the roll axis, to apply to a sloshing study of your own tanks.

The study ran two-dimensional cases in OpenFOAM v2312 (December 2023; interFoam, volume of fluid). The benchmark tank is rectangular, 1 m long and 1 m high. In each free-decay run, a small first-mode wave rings down in the still tank, and its frequency is read from the record. Fill is the still-water depth as a share of the tank height. The analysis results for the two-dimensional tank case are compared to the available public literature. At fills of 15, 30, 50 and 70 %, the largest difference from linear theory is 0.30 %, at 30 % fill, where CFD is below theory. Refining the mesh from 1,600 to 25,600 cells moves that difference only from 0.326 % to 0.296 %. It comes mainly from the finite height of the starting wave, not from the mesh.

The resonance moves with roll angle and roll axis

Forced-roll runs drive a 0.9 m tank at 1 to 8° roll over a range of periods. They find the resonance from the phase of the tank's response (Bäuerlein and Avila 2021). At 30 % and 70 % fill, the resonant frequency falls as roll grows: about 1 to 4 % below the formula at 2°, and about 11 to 13 % below at 8°. These shifts come from runs of six roll cycles, which the study reads as lower bounds: a 12-cycle run gives a larger shift. At 15 % fill the resonance sits above the formula instead: 7.5 % above at 1°, and 1.2 % above at 8°, which is within the sweep's resolution. There the water crosses the tank as a breaking bore and runs up the wall to about three times its still depth at 8°.

Where the roll axis sits below the tank, for example for a tank high in the hull, the tank is also pushed sideways as it rolls (Kapsenberg and Carette 2023). At 70 % fill and 4° roll, rolling the tank about its own floor puts the resonance about 10 % below the formula, in a 12-cycle run; the 6-cycle point for 70 % fill and 4° in the chart below reads 8 %. With the axis half a tank length below the floor, the resonance sits about 16 % below; with the axis one tank length below, about 20 % below. The study quotes these shifts to the nearest percent. Peak wall run-up is about 15 % higher with the axis below the floor.

Tank damping has an optimum

The forced-roll runs at 4° also give each fill's equivalent damping ratio: 0.095 at 30 % fill, 0.18 at 50 % and 0.305 at 70 %. These feed a screening step with the classic tuned-absorber equations used for anti-roll tanks (Moaleji and Greig 2007), at a tank-to-vessel mass ratio of 0.05, with perfect tuning and no hull damping. The best damping ratio is then 0.13. At 30 % fill the tank is under-damped and splits the vessel's roll response into two peaks. At 70 % fill it is over-damped and the peak grows again. The 50 % fill gives the lowest roll peak of the three.

Run time on one workstation

Each case runs on one core. A batch of 48 forced-roll cases ran 14 at a time on one 32-core workstation in 34.7 min, against an estimated 247 to 336 min one at a time.

Appendix A Reusable prompts

This prompt lets your team, or any assistant, recompute the theory side of our sloshing benchmark, compare it with our published CFD values, and read the resonance shifts at 8° roll from our archived data. With OpenFOAM, it also reruns a CFD case. To have a study of your own tanks done for you instead, see below.

What you need to run this

  • Data required: None to reproduce the benchmark;
  • Data required to adapt: tank length, fill depth and shape, your vessel's roll period, the roll angle of interest and the roll axis height.
  • Paid or behind an account: nothing. OpenFOAM is free, open-source software.
  • Changes over time: nothing. The CFD outputs are archived at a fixed commit.

Reproduce the published result

Give this prompt to your assistant:

Using the study at
https://vamseeachanta.github.io/digitalmodel/cfd/sloshing-cfd-study.html
and our public code repository https://github.com/vamseeachanta/digitalmodel
at commit 705857af62385a4567c96be99a129cdff3051a52 (MIT licence),
with Python 3.11:
1. Recompute the theory side and compare with our published CFD values.
   For a two-dimensional rectangular tank of length L and fill depth h,
   linear theory gives the first-mode frequency as
   Omega1 = omega1 * sqrt(L / g) = sqrt(pi * tanh(pi * h / L)).
   Compute it at h/L = 0.15, 0.30, 0.50 and 0.70. Compare it with the
   published CFD values 1.1779, 1.5164, 1.6974 and 1.7508, archived in
   docs/api/structural/sloshing-cfd-benchmark.json at that commit.
   Report each difference in %, signed: (CFD - theory) / theory.
   Check: theory 1.1746, 1.5210, 1.6974 and 1.7508; differences +0.28 %,
   -0.30 %, 0.00 % and 0.00 % (CFD is below theory at h/L = 0.30).
2. Read docs/api/structural/sloshing-backbone.json at that commit.
   Report detuning_pct at 8 deg roll for h/L = 0.30 and 0.70.
   Check: -10.81 % and -13.25 %.
3. Only if I have OpenFOAM v2312 on Linux, rerun the free-decay cases
   (about 10 min per case) at that commit:
     git clone --filter=blob:none https://github.com/vamseeachanta/digitalmodel
     cd digitalmodel
     git checkout 705857af62385a4567c96be99a129cdff3051a52
     source /usr/lib/openfoam/openfoam2312/etc/bashrc
     uv venv --python 3.11
     uv pip install --no-sources -e .
     .venv/bin/python scripts/cfd/run_sloshing_benchmark.py free-decay --work-dir ./sloshing-work
   The run rewrites docs/api/structural/sloshing-cfd-benchmark.json, so
   compare its new values with the step 1 check values above, not with the
   file itself.
