THE IDEA BEHIND EVERYTHING HERE

Start with the organisation you want to become

Helping organisations reach their goals and become more successful starts with the outcome: which work matters, what could change, and what evidence would demonstrate progress.

Agents create room to reconsider how work is done. A useful starting point is four kinds of ambition: Speed, Altitude, Depth and Range.

Speed: Complete the same work in less time.

Altitude: Move up a level of abstraction, from an individual task to the outcome it serves.

Depth: Approach a task as a leading expert would, working deeper into the underlying data, tools and problems.

Range: Bring in knowledge, tools and techniques from several fields beyond the task’s usual discipline.

Speed: an installation contractor could set a goal of running its own installation analysis in-house within 24 hours. Depth: it could retain lookup tables and databases from operations, then investigate how those records could support live operations.

Altitude: an engineering firm could aim to take on more of its work by improving an existing calculation or reporting process. The goal is greater capacity, measured through elapsed time, rework and review findings.

These examples are goals. Operational use requires qualified inputs, checked methods and an accountable engineer.

SHORT READ

Ask big, then check the work

Depth starts with the underlying inputs and methods. Give your assistant an outcome, a bounded scope and a checkable definition of done. Watch and check while the task runs at first: inspect the inputs, the first calculation and the saved output.

For a long task, set a checkpoint and check back in an hour. Look for completed cases, recorded exceptions and results read back from the actual output. Adapt that interval to the task.

Engineering stays precise and consistent through fixed units, governing criteria, repeatable checks and independent review. Keep a progress record so the next session can resume from evidence. The linked practice note describes progress records and end-to-end tests.

MAIN STREET: WHAT WE SET UP THIS MONTH

E-mail authentication

Done, 17 October 2026.

E-mail authentication records were added on 17 October: SPF, DKIM and DMARC. They help receiving systems identify authorised mail and reduce spoofing and spam placement; they do not establish inbox delivery for each message.

Do it yourself

  1. Inventory the services authorised to send your company mail.
  2. Add the authentication records through the mail and domain administration tools.
  3. Check the published records and inspect authentication results on a test message.

One decisions page

Done, 17 October 2026.

One page gathers every open decision with a recommendation. The maintainer’s answers are saved in one click.

Do it yourself

  1. Gather open decisions into one page, each with the recommended action and its reason.
  2. Provide a way to record the maintainer’s answer with the decision.
  3. Test an answer, reload the page and check that the recorded decision remains.

Contact list cleanup

Done, 17 October 2026.

Duplicate contact entries were merged and consent was noted before the first send. A contact entry is not permission to send; only the recorded consent governs inclusion.

Do it yourself

  1. Choose one authoritative contact register and compare duplicate entries.
  2. Merge duplicates without losing the consent record or any stop instruction.
  3. Inspect the proposed export against recorded consent before a separately approved send.

MAIN STREET EDITION

Automate the routine admin

Choose the organisational goal first: fewer administrative interruptions or more time for engineering. This is Speed applied to routine work. Start with inputs and approval rules that can be written down. OpenAI’s gpt-realtime live voice release of 28 August 2025 added phone integration and remote tool connections. A caller’s request can become a draft task that someone checks and approves. If your phone or accounting tool includes these features, the steps below provide a starting point.

OpenAI live voice release - 28 August 2025

The steps: capture > check > approve.

Call handling

A spoken request can become a routed call or a draft follow-up task.

Do it yourself:

1. Write a one-page routing table: topics, covering team, opening hours and backup.

2. Enable speech recognition or live voice in the phone system; test routine and unclear requests.

3. Inspect the transcript and task record, check consent and retention settings, and keep a manual route.

Invoicing

Job records can supply the fields for a draft invoice.

Do it yourself:

1. Choose a completed job and the approved quote as the source of customer, items and rates.

2. Enable draft invoices in the accounting tool; compare quantities, tax and totals with the source.

3. Approve a small checked sample before enabling reminders; keep invoice sending under approval.

Timesheets

A shared form can feed a checked weekly summary.

Do it yourself:

1. Create a form for date, job code and hours, using the existing rounding and overtime rules.

2. Connect it to a spreadsheet; flag missing days and totals outside the agreed limits.

3. Reconcile one week with the source records, then approve the payroll or job-cost export.

Expense reports

Receipt extraction can populate a draft expense line.

Do it yourself:

1. Use the receipt capture feature in the existing card or accounting app.

2. Check date, supplier, tax and total against the image; match the line to the card statement.

3. Apply the expense policy, route exceptions for review, and approve the checked export.
