Analysis method

Potential-flow diffraction analysis describes a floating hull's six-degree-of-freedom motion response amplitude operators (RAOs) across wave frequency and heading: surge, sway, heave, roll, pitch and yaw. Amplitude and phase require consistent units and phase conventions.

Model basis and interpretation

A report basis identifies geometry, mass, loading condition, restoring properties, mesh, water depth, wave headings, frequency grid and damping treatment. Each input requires a public source or an explicitly generic definition before results can be published here.

Radiation damping alone excludes external or viscous roll damping. Roll-resonance response depends on damping treatment; an undamped potential-flow peak is not an operability prediction. Frequency-band coverage and mesh resolution constrain interpretation.

The long-period check compares translational response with wave-following limits and rotational response with wave-slope limits. In the long-wave limit in deep water, surge follows the cosine of wave heading and sway follows its sine; heave approaches wave elevation. Roll and pitch approach the corresponding wave-slope components, while yaw tends toward zero. A finite-period comparison requires the stated heading, units and convergence criterion.

Reliability flags identify response bands that require review. Flagged rows are excluded from automatic summary statistics. Phase conventions require alignment before cross-tool comparison.

Comparison method

Magnitude comparisons use the overlapping period band at common headings, with linear interpolation of the second series onto the first series' period grid. Peak differences compare each series' own peak within that band. Maximum absolute differences and root-mean-square differences summarize the overlapping response.

Percentage differences use the first series as the reference and are suppressed where its magnitude is below 5 % of its peak, to avoid ratios dominated by near-zero values. Rows with unresolved reliability flags are excluded from comparison statistics.

Comparisons require consistent hull geometry, loading, restoring properties, damping, phase conventions and wave conditions. Comparisons between different hulls require separate interpretation of geometry and loading effects. Changes in damping treatment are assessed as damping sensitivity.

Publication basis

No numerical analysis results are presented on this index. Publication requires traceable public input particulars and sources, or a documented generic geometry and analysis basis.
